# [Arabic Rhetorical Device Taxonomy (v0.1.1)](https://github.com/Al-Balagha/Arabic-Rhetoric/tree/v0.1.1)
### A project of the [Encyclopedia of Arabic Rhetoric](https://al-balagha.com)

The systematic, hierarchical classification of **Arabic rhetorical devices** published by the [Encyclopedia of Arabic Rhetoric](https://al-balagha.com).

  * **Moved**
  	* [CG-1: Rhetorical Shift](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/CG-1:_Rhetorical_Shift) from [Domain C: Linguistic Embellishments (‘ilm al-badī‘)](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Arabic_Rhetoric#C) to [Domain A: Sentence Construction & Syntactical Stylistics (‘ilm al-ma‘ānī)](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Arabic_Rhetoric#A), to better reflect its semantic and syntactic mechanisms.<br>&nbsp;

  * **Split**
	* [Domain A: Sentence Construction & Syntactical Stylistics (‘ilm al-ma‘ānī)](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Arabic_Rhetoric#A) into four thematic subcategories: [(1) Sentence-Type Switches](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Sentence-Type_Switches), [(2) Referential Switches](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Referential_Switches), [(3) Information Flow & Sequencing](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Information_Flow_%26_Sequencing), and [(4) Textual Additions & Omissions](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Textual_Additions_%26_Omissions).<br>&nbsp;
  	* [Domain B: Figurative Speech (‘ilm al-bayān)](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Arabic_Rhetoric#B) into three thematic subcategories: [(1) Explicit Comparison](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Explicit_Comparison), [(2) Conceptual Transfer](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Conceptual_Transfer), and [(3) Semantic Ambiguity](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Semantic_Ambiguity).<br>&nbsp;
	* [Argumentation & Illustrative Persuasion](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Argumentation_%26_Illustrative_Persuasion) into two thematic subcategories: [Intertextuality](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Intertextuality) and [Argumentation & Illustrative Persuasion](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/Category:Argumentation_%26_Illustrative_Persuasion).<br>&nbsp;
	* [A-8: Definiteness and Indefiniteness](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/A-8:_Definiteness_and_Indefiniteness) into two distinct devices: [DEFINT: Definiteness](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/DEFINT:_Definiteness) and [INDEF: Indefiniteness](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/INDEF:_Indefiniteness).<br>&nbsp;
  	* [A-11: Conjunction and Disjunction](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/A-11:_Conjunction_and_Disjunction) into two distinct devices: [CONJUN: Conjunction](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/CONJUN:_Conjunction) and [DISJUN: Disjunction](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/DISJUN:_Disjunction).<br>&nbsp;
   	* [A-13: Brevity, Verbosity and Moderation](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/A-13:_Brevity,_Verbosity_and_Moderation) into two distinct devices: [BREVTY: Brevity](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/BREVTY:_Brevity) and [VERBOS: Verbosity](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/VERBOS:_Verbosity).<br>Dropped "Moderation" as a rhetorical device, as it is considered the baseline for [BREVTY: Brevity](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/BREVTY:_Brevity) and [VERBOS: Verbosity](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/VERBOS:_Verbosity) rather than an independent, identifiable device.<br>&nbsp;
	* [CG-4: Quotation & Poetic Incorporation](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/CG-4:_Quotation_%26_Poetic_Incorporation) into two distinct devices: [ISLQUOT: Islamic Quotation](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/ISLQUOT:_Islamic_Quotation) and [POETINC: Poetic Incorporation](https://al-balagha.com/wiki/POETINC:_Poetic_Incorporation).<br>&nbsp;

  * **Removed**
  	* [Latin and Greek synonyms for rhetorical devices](https://blog.al-balagha.com/removal-of-latin-greek-synonyms-for-rhetorical-devices/).<br>&nbsp;

## 🔢 Changelog
🏷️ **2025-11-20 - [v0.1.1](https://github.com/Al-Balagha/Arabic-Rhetoric/tree/v0.1.1) (Machine-readable formats added)**  
This update introduces full machine-readable exports of the Arabic Rhetorical Device Taxonomy to support digital humanities research, linked-data workflows, and reproducible computational analysis.

Added in this release:

* A new **machine-readable/** directory containing:
	* [JSON](machine-readable/json)
	* [JSON-LD](machine-readable/jsonld)
	* [RDF/XML](machine-readable/rdfxml)
	* [TTL](machine-readable/ttl)
* Each file corresponds to a single rhetorical device (Q-item) from [**BalaghaBase.org**](https://balaghabase.org), the project's custom Wikibase instance.  
* Formats are aligned with semantic web standards and suitable for ingestion into digital humanities tools, corpus annotation systems, and knowledge graphs.  

This marks the first structured data release of the taxonomy, expanding its use beyond human-readable PDF/HTML formats into machine-actionable forms.

---
🏷️ **2025-11-17 - [v0.1.0](https://github.com/Al-Balagha/Arabic-Rhetoric/tree/v0.1.0) (First public release)**  
This is the first public, versioned release of the Arabic Rhetorical Device Taxonomy, making three years of iterative development openly available on [GitHub](https://github.com/Al-Balagha/Arabic-Rhetoric).

Included in this release:

* Complete taxonomy of 95 rhetorical devices across Domains A–D.
* Full [PDF](/PDF/) and [HTML](/HTML/) snapshot exports from the [Encyclopedia of Arabic Rhetoric](https://al-balagha.com).
* [A combined PDF of all devices for offline use](Encyclopedia_of_Arabic_Rhetoric_FULL.pdf).
* CITATION.cff, [README](README.md), LICENSE, and [taxonomy-index](taxonomy-index.md).
* Stable structure suitable for long-term citation and Zenodo archiving.

🏷️ **2022-09-06 - Initial draft (pre-release)**  
The first complete draft of the taxonomy was created and submitted as part of the [author's](https://marathe.org) [Masters dissertation](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.21106) at the University of Exeter. The taxonomy was created to assist in accurately identifying Arabic rhetorical devices in texts, as part of the author's [research project](https://project.balaghascore.com) to measure the [rhetorical density](https://rhetorical-density.com) of texts. 

* Rhetorical density is a novel quantitative linguistics metric devised by the author, based on the relative frequency analysis of rhetorical devices. It enables comparative measurement of rhetoricity across texts and genres. It is [defined](https://rhetorical-density.com/#what) as the number of rhetorical devices per 100 orthographic words or word units.

This early version of the taxonomy formed the conceptual foundation for identifying Arabic rhetorical devices consistently, and for building a stable ontology for rhetorical analysis.
